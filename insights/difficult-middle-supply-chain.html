OWNING THE DIFFICULT MIDDLE · CASE STUDY

From an idea to a new supply chain.

The difficult middle is the space between a boardroom idea and a working business reality. This was one of those jobs: challenge an established European supply chain, build an alternative and make it business as usual.

Delivery.

A plan on paper was only the beginning. Over roughly six months, the work meant getting on planes to China multiple times, travelling into Vietnam, developing suppliers, employing local capability and establishing an office presence in China. The formal company setup was completed locally; the job was to make the commercial model happen.

The objective was not simply to buy cheaper. It was to create a credible, controlled alternative to an established European supply chain — improving the bottom line while creating opportunities to pass an element of the benefit to clients and strengthen the market proposition.

Most importantly, it could not remain a project. The new approach had to be integrated into normal operations: a new BAU.

From concept
to BAU.

Five practical moves took the idea from a commercial opportunity to an operating supply route.

01

Build the commercial case

Identify where the established model constrained margin, competitiveness or opportunity, and turn that into a practical sourcing plan.

02

Go and make it real

Meet suppliers, understand capability first-hand, test assumptions and develop the relationships needed to move from presentation to procurement.

03

Create capability on the ground

Employ local resource and establish an office presence in China so the model had structure rather than depending on occasional buying trips.

04

Shift the supply chain

Move appropriate sourcing from the established European model into China and Vietnam while keeping commercial control and delivery requirements central.

05

Turn change into BAU

Capture bottom-line improvement, create client-facing savings opportunities and embed the new supply route into normal operations.
